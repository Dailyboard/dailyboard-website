<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="dailyboard-on-your-phone">Dailyboard on your phone</h2>

Your board now adapts to a small screen with a focused view of one person at a time. Switch between people using the participant selector or a swipe, and move through dates without squeezing a desktop-sized board onto your phone.

Tap a commitment to open its details in a panel from the bottom of the screen. You can expand it for more room, update the status or due date, change assignees, and edit the description, notes, or link. Visible Save and Cancel actions make text editing easier with a touch keyboard.

<img src="/images/updates/2026-10-19/mobile-board.png" alt="Dailyboard on a phone: a shared Product team board with a participant selector and Emma’s commitments for today and tomorrow." width="390" height="760" loading="lazy" style="max-width:390px;width:100%;height:auto;margin:2rem auto;border-radius:12px" />

Navigation has also been brought together across pages. The menu, page context and board controls stay accessible while you scroll, and switching organizations uses the same familiar menu. When the current week is visible, tap its date range to scroll back to today.

These improvements are available in Dailyboard on the mobile web. Open Dailyboard in your phone's browser to try them.

<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="give-overdue-commitments-a-next-step">Give overdue commitments a next step</h2>

Some commitments are waiting for a date. Others have a date that has already passed. The Inbox now brings those two situations together with **Backlog** and **Overdue** tabs, on desktop and mobile.

Backlog holds work to plan later. Overdue shows unfinished commitments assigned to you with a due date before today, using your board's timezone. A count and indicator help you spot work that needs attention without searching through previous weeks.

<img src="/images/updates/2026-10-19/inbox.png" alt="Dailyboard Inbox showing the Backlog and Overdue tabs on a demonstration board." width="390" height="760" loading="lazy" style="max-width:390px;width:100%;height:auto;margin:2rem auto;border-radius:12px" />

<img src="/images/updates/2026-10-19/desktop-inbox.png" alt="Desktop Dailyboard with the Overdue drawer beside the weekly board and two demonstration commitments waiting for a new plan." width="1200" height="560" loading="lazy" />

Give an overdue commitment a new date, or use the bulk actions to mark the list Done or Not done. On desktop, drag an overdue item onto today or a future date on the board. On a phone, open the commitment's details to choose its next date. Bulk updates now show progress while they're being saved.

**Where to find it:** open the Inbox from your board's header. On desktop, the **B** and **O** shortcuts open Backlog and Overdue respectively.

<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="forgot-your-password-you-can-reset-it">Forgot your password? You can reset it.</h2>

This was a frequent request, and it's now available: you can recover access to your Dailyboard account yourself.

Choose **Forgot password?** on the login screen, enter your email address, and follow the link in the email to set a new password. Then sign in and pick up where you left off. The recovery link can be used once and expires after 30 minutes; if needed, request a new one.

<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="fewer-interruptions-more-reliable-workdays">Fewer interruptions, more reliable workdays</h2>

We've made reducing errors a sustained part of our work. Your feedback about recurring errors and speed led to a focused effort to investigate problems, fix their causes, and make everyday flows more resilient.

That includes fixes for cloning commitments, expired sessions, live board updates, and recovery when an open browser tab still has files from an older release. Repeated error messages are now grouped and limited, so one problem doesn't bury your screen under a stack of duplicate alerts.

We've also optimized board and commitment reads to reduce repeated database work, batched updates when refreshing commitments, and improved how large overdue status changes are saved. These are concrete steps toward a smoother experience, especially as boards and lists grow. Performance remains an ongoing priority.

Behind those changes, we've improved error detection and introduced recurring reviews of production problems, with follow-up fixes and checks after deployment. The goal is simple: catch problems sooner, respond faster, and let you spend more of your day working instead of dealing with Dailyboard errors.

<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="when-someone-leaves-your-organization">When someone leaves your organization</h2>

People who manage an organization can now remove a member from the **People** page. Open the person's actions menu and choose Remove. The confirmation explains what will happen before you proceed.

Removing a member revokes their organization access and removes their assignments while keeping manually created commitments. It also disconnects their calendar integration for that organization and **permanently deletes imports owned by that integration**. Check that consequence before confirming, particularly if those imported events are shared with other people.

<h2 style="scroll-margin-top:6rem" id="also-improved">Also improved</h2>

- Newly created backlog commitments appear at the top, so they're easy to find.
- The due-date popover has a visible Save button; Enter still works too.
- Participant avatars fit more reliably on cards, with a clear count when more people are assigned.
- We've fixed overlapping menus, mobile logout, and saving a board with no participants selected.
- New accounts verify their email address before entering Dailyboard. Existing accounts keep workspace access, but an unverified address needs confirmation before accepting another organization invitation.
- Other bug fixes and improvements help make the everyday experience more consistent.

Thanks for telling us where Dailyboard gets in your way. Keep sharing what feels slow, confusing, or unreliable — that feedback helps us decide what to improve next.
